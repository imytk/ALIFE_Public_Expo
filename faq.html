Frequently Asked Questions

About the Expo

What is this?

The Artificial Life Online Expo is a digital version of a physical exhibition first shown at the ALIFE 2025 conference in Kyoto, Japan. It's a guided tour through nine zones, each exploring a different idea in Artificial Life (ALife) — from the basic question of what "life" even means, through emergence, evolution, and complexity, to the diverse forms ALife takes and the questions it raises about the future. Every zone mixes short explanatory text with a hands-on simulation you can try directly in your browser.

Why did you make this?

The original exhibition only ran for the few days of the conference, and most people who'd benefit from it were never in the room. This online version exists so anyone curious about ALife can wander through it at their own pace, whenever they like, from anywhere — and it gives us a chance to keep improving it long after the conference ended.

Who made it?

The expo is curated by Imy Khan (Lead Curator & Exhibition Designer), with contributions from a number of people across the ALife community. You'll find the full list of contributors on the homepage.

Is this an official resource from ISAL, or the wider ALife community?

Not officially, no. This expo was created independently by Imy Khan, who also serves as the Education & Social Outreach Chair for the International Society for Artificial Life (ISAL). It was first shown at ALIFE 2025, the conference associated with ISAL, but it isn't an ISAL publication — think of it as a labour of love from someone embedded in the community, rather than an institutional project.

How is this different from just reading about ALife on Wikipedia?

Wikipedia is a great reference, but it isn't built to be explored hands-on. This expo pairs short, plain-language explanations with live simulations you can actually play with — watch a flock of digital boids self-organise, evolve a population of virtual creatures, or run Conway's Game of Life yourself — so the ideas click in a way that's harder to get from text alone.

Visiting

Do I need any background in science or programming to follow along?

No. The expo was originally built for the general public, so it assumes no prior knowledge. If you can be curious, you're qualified.

Do I have to go through the zones in order?

Not at all. The zones are numbered because that's the order visitors experienced them at the physical exhibition, and later zones do build gently on ideas from earlier ones — but you're free to jump straight to whatever looks most interesting from the homepage.

Does this work on my phone?

Yes, the site itself is mobile-friendly. Some of the embedded simulations are built by outside researchers and creators and weren't necessarily designed with small screens in mind, so a couple may be easier to use on a laptop or desktop.

Is this appropriate for classrooms or kids?

Broadly yes — the tone and content are aimed at a general public audience with no assumed background. That said, we haven't specifically vetted every external simulation for a classroom setting, so if you're planning to use this with younger students, we'd recommend previewing a zone first.

The Simulations

Some simulations just link out to another website instead of loading here — why?

A handful of the older or more complex simulations rely on technology that modern browsers can no longer run inside an embedded frame (old Java applets, for example). In those cases, we link directly to wherever you can actually try them instead of pretending they work here.

Does the embedded Google Form (or other interactives) collect my personal data?

Where we embed a Google Form, it's collecting whatever you choose to submit through it (like your response to a survey question), governed by Google's own privacy policy — we don't separately track or store that data ourselves. Third-party simulations linked or embedded from other sites are run by their original creators, so their own privacy practices apply, not ours.

Contributing

Can I use some of this material?

Reach out via the Contact page and we'll figure out what's possible — it depends on which part of the material you mean, since some content (like our own placard text) is easier to share than others (like third-party simulations, which have their own separate licenses).

You forgot a zone about [X]!

Nine zones can't cover everything — ALife is a huge field. If there's a topic, simulation, or idea you think deserves a spot, we'd genuinely love to hear about it. Reach out via the Contact page.

Can I translate this into another language?

Yes, and we'd love the help. The site currently supports English and Japanese, and we're actively looking for volunteer translators to add more. Reach out via the Contact page if you're interested.

I found a bug, a typo, or something's not working — what do I do?

Please tell us. Head to the Contact page, or check the Change Log first to see if it's already a known issue.

Is there a way to cite this in a paper or presentation?

Get in touch via the Contact page and we can point you to the right citation format — this hasn't been formally set up yet, so we'd rather work it out with you directly than give you something that turns out to be wrong.
